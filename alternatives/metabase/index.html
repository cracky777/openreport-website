Open Report vs Metabase

This one is not a story about escaping a vendor: Metabase is open source, self-hostable and considerably more mature than Open Report, with a large community behind it. If you are weighing the two, the question is not licensing but shape: what a report is meant to look like, and who is meant to build it.

Where Metabase is stronger

No tool wins everywhere, and a comparison that pretends otherwise is not worth reading.

  • Years of production use, a large community, and answers already written for most problems you will hit.
  • Many more database connectors.
  • A question-asking experience designed for people who do not write SQL, refined over a long time.
  • A commercial edition and a support ecosystem for organisations that need one.

Where Open Report fits better

  • Reports are composed on a free canvas rather than a grid: multi-page documents, pixel placement, merged frames. Closer to a report than to a dashboard.
  • Row-level security and custom SQL measures live on the shared model, so every report inherits them.
  • Scheduled emails carry the report as a PDF attachment, not a link to go and look.
  • The managed Cloud runs in France, for teams that need European hosting.

Try it yourself

Self-hosting takes one command from a fresh clone. The installer checks your Node version and installs it if needed. Or open the hosted app and skip the server entirely.
